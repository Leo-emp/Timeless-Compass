# Fact sheet: salamis_480bc

Narrator lines cite these fact ids: `[src: f-...]`. Disputed facts must be told as "Herodotus says...", never stated flat.

- `f-date` The battle of Salamis was fought in 480 BC, traditionally dated to late September.
  - sources: Barry Strauss, The Battle of Salamis (2004); Peter Green, The Greco-Persian Wars (1996)
- `f-susa` Xerxes sent a horseman to Susa to announce his success.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.54
- `f-aeschylus-message` Aeschylus, eight years later, also tells of a Greek who came to Xerxes saying the Greeks would slip away under cover of night.
  - sources: Aeschylus, The Persians 355-363
- `f-throne-aigaleos` Xerxes watched the battle seated at the foot of Mount Aigaleos, on the mainland opposite Salamis.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.90
- `f-throne-scribes` Whenever he saw one of his captains do something notable, he asked who it was, and his scribes wrote down the captain's name, his father and his city.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.90
- `f-throne-golden` Plutarch says Xerxes sat on a golden throne, with many secretaries beside him to record the battle.
  - sources: Plutarch, Life of Themistocles 13
- `f-artemisia` Artemisia, queen of Halicarnassus, chased by an Athenian ship, rammed and sank a ship from her own side.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.87
- `f-men-women` Xerxes thought she had sunk a Greek ship and said: my men have become women, and my women men.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.88
- `f-phoenicians` When Phoenician captains blamed the Ionians for the defeat, Xerxes had them beheaded.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.90
- `f-robes` Aeschylus says that when Xerxes saw the depth of the disaster he tore his robes and cried out, for he had a seat in full view of the whole army, on a high hill near the sea.
  - sources: Aeschylus, The Persians 465-470
- `f-narrows` In the narrow strait the crowded Persian ships could not use their numbers; they struck each other with their bronze rams and broke their oars.
  - sources: Aeschylus, The Persians 412-420; Herodotus, Histories 8.86
- `f-fleet-size` **(disputed)** Aeschylus puts the Greek fleet at about 310 ships and Xerxes' fleet at 1,207.
  - sources: Aeschylus, The Persians 338-343; Barry Strauss, The Battle of Salamis (2004)
  - note: Herodotus gives 378 Greek ships; modern historians think the Persian fleet was far smaller than 1,207 by the day of battle. Say 'Aeschylus claims'.
