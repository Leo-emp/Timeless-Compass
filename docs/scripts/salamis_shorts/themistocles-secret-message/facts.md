# Fact sheet: salamis_480bc

Narrator lines cite these fact ids: `[src: f-...]`. Disputed facts must be told as "Herodotus says...", never stated flat.

- `f-date` The battle of Salamis was fought in 480 BC, traditionally dated to late September.
  - sources: Barry Strauss, The Battle of Salamis (2004); Peter Green, The Greco-Persian Wars (1996)
- `f-panic` When news reached the Greek fleet at Salamis that the Acropolis had fallen, some commanders panicked and voted to sail away to the Isthmus.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.56
- `f-trap-why` Themistocles sent the message when he was losing the council debate to the Peloponnesian commanders, who wanted to leave Salamis and fight at the Isthmus.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.74-75; Plutarch, Life of Themistocles 12
- `f-sicinnus-who` Sicinnus was a slave of Themistocles' household and the tutor of his children.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.75; Plutarch, Life of Themistocles 12
- `f-sicinnus-message` Themistocles sent him by boat to the Persian commanders with a message: Themistocles was on the king's side, and the Greeks were terrified and planning to flee.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.75; Plutarch, Life of Themistocles 12
- `f-sicinnus-bait` The message said that if the Persians stopped the Greeks escaping, they would see them fighting among themselves.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.75
- `f-aeschylus-message` Aeschylus, eight years later, also tells of a Greek who came to Xerxes saying the Greeks would slip away under cover of night.
  - sources: Aeschylus, The Persians 355-363
- `f-night-move` The Persians believed it: that night they moved their ships to close the channels around Salamis and landed soldiers on the islet of Psyttaleia.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.76; Aeschylus, The Persians 364-373
- `f-aristides` Aristides, Themistocles' old rival who had been ostracised from Athens, crossed from Aegina and brought word that the fleet was surrounded.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.79-81; Plutarch, Life of Themistocles 12
- `f-sicinnus-reward` Afterwards Themistocles made Sicinnus a citizen of Thespiae and a rich man.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.75
- `f-trap` Modern historians read the message as a deliberate trap to force the battle into the narrow strait.
  - sources: Barry Strauss, The Battle of Salamis (2004); Peter Green, The Greco-Persian Wars (1996); Tom Holland, Persian Fire (2005)
- `f-narrows` In the narrow strait the crowded Persian ships could not use their numbers; they struck each other with their bronze rams and broke their oars.
  - sources: Aeschylus, The Persians 412-420; Herodotus, Histories 8.86
