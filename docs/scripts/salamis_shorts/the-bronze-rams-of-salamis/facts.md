# Fact sheet: salamis_480bc

Narrator lines cite these fact ids: `[src: f-...]`. Disputed facts must be told as "Herodotus says...", never stated flat.

- `f-date` The battle of Salamis was fought in 480 BC, traditionally dated to late September.
  - sources: Barry Strauss, The Battle of Salamis (2004); Peter Green, The Greco-Persian Wars (1996)
- `f-laurium` Years earlier Themistocles had persuaded Athens to spend a rich new silver strike from the Laurium mines on warships instead of sharing the money out.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 7.144; Plutarch, Life of Themistocles 4
- `f-athens-ships` Athens supplied the largest contingent of the Greek fleet: 180 ships.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.44
- `f-trap` Modern historians read the message as a deliberate trap to force the battle into the narrow strait.
  - sources: Barry Strauss, The Battle of Salamis (2004); Peter Green, The Greco-Persian Wars (1996); Tom Holland, Persian Fire (2005)
- `f-artemisia` Artemisia, queen of Halicarnassus, chased by an Athenian ship, rammed and sank a ship from her own side.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.87
- `f-ram-weapon` A trireme's main weapon was a bronze ram fixed at the waterline of its bow.
  - sources: J. S. Morrison, J. F. Coates and N. B. Rankov, The Athenian Trireme (2nd ed.) (2000)
- `f-rowers` A trireme carried about 170 rowers on three levels and about 200 men in all.
  - sources: J. S. Morrison, J. F. Coates and N. B. Rankov, The Athenian Trireme (2nd ed.) (2000); Herodotus, Histories 7.184
- `f-first-ram` Herodotus says the Athenian Ameinias of Pallene was the first to ram an enemy ship; when the ships locked together, others came to help and the battle began.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.84
- `f-aeschylus-ram` Aeschylus says a Greek ship began the ramming and smashed off the curved stern of a Phoenician ship.
  - sources: Aeschylus, The Persians 408-411
- `f-narrows` In the narrow strait the crowded Persian ships could not use their numbers; they struck each other with their bronze rams and broke their oars.
  - sources: Aeschylus, The Persians 412-420; Herodotus, Histories 8.86
- `f-fleet-size` **(disputed)** Aeschylus puts the Greek fleet at about 310 ships and Xerxes' fleet at 1,207.
  - sources: Aeschylus, The Persians 338-343; Barry Strauss, The Battle of Salamis (2004)
  - note: Herodotus gives 378 Greek ships; modern historians think the Persian fleet was far smaller than 1,207 by the day of battle. Say 'Aeschylus claims'.
- `f-athlit` The largest ancient warship ram ever found, the Athlit ram, was discovered off Israel in 1980: one piece of cast bronze, about 2.26 metres long and 465 kilograms. It comes from a later warship, not from Salamis.
  - sources: L. Casson and J. R. Steffy (eds.), The Athlit Ram (1991)
