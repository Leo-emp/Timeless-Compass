# Fact sheet: salamis_480bc

Narrator lines cite these fact ids: `[src: f-...]`. Disputed facts must be told as "Herodotus says...", never stated flat.

- `f-date` The battle of Salamis was fought in 480 BC, traditionally dated to late September.
  - sources: Barry Strauss, The Battle of Salamis (2004); Peter Green, The Greco-Persian Wars (1996)
- `f-oracle-first` Before the invasion, Athenian envoys at Delphi received a terrifying first answer from the priestess Aristonice: flee to the ends of the earth.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 7.140
- `f-oracle-second` The envoys returned as suppliants carrying olive branches and were given a second answer: Zeus grants that a wooden wall alone shall not be taken.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 7.141
- `f-oracle-salamis` The second answer also said: divine Salamis, you will destroy the children of women.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 7.141
- `f-oracle-hedge` Some older Athenians believed the wooden wall meant the thorn hedge that once ringed the Acropolis.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 7.142
- `f-oracle-ships` Others said the wooden wall meant the ships, and Themistocles argued the oracle would have said 'cruel Salamis', not 'divine', if Athenians were meant to die there.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 7.142-143; Plutarch, Life of Themistocles 10
- `f-oracle-vote` The Athenians decided to meet the invader at sea with their ships, together with the other Greeks who were willing.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 7.144; Barry Strauss, The Battle of Salamis (2004)
- `f-laurium` Years earlier Themistocles had persuaded Athens to spend a rich new silver strike from the Laurium mines on warships instead of sharing the money out.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 7.144; Plutarch, Life of Themistocles 4
- `f-laurium-number` **(disputed)** Herodotus says the silver built 200 ships.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 7.144; Plutarch, Life of Themistocles 4
  - note: Herodotus says 200 ships; Plutarch and Aristotle's Constitution of the Athenians say 100. Say 'a new fleet' or give both numbers.
- `f-evacuation` The Athenians evacuated their families and slaves to Troezen, Aegina and Salamis.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.41; Plutarch, Life of Themistocles 10
- `f-defenders` A few Athenians, the temple stewards and poor men, stayed on the Acropolis behind a barricade of planks and timber, believing it was the wooden wall of the oracle.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.51
- `f-burned` They killed the defenders, plundered the temple and set the whole Acropolis on fire.
  - sources: Herodotus, Histories 8.53
- `f-narrows` In the narrow strait the crowded Persian ships could not use their numbers; they struck each other with their bronze rams and broke their oars.
  - sources: Aeschylus, The Persians 412-420; Herodotus, Histories 8.86
