## Main character
Themistocles of Athens — distrusted, clever, out of time — seen through the eyes of the king he is about to trap.

## Other characters
Xerxes (wants Greece to surrender, watching from a golden throne with scribes ready) · Sicinnus (the slave who
carries the secret message across the water at night).

## Want
Force the battle into the narrow strait, where the Persian numbers stop mattering.

## Fear
The Greek allies sail away in the night and Athens' people, crowded on Salamis, are left to be slaughtered.

## Choice
Send the enemy a secret message that betrays his own side's plans — to trap the king into attacking.

## Central question
How did one man trick the largest navy on Earth into rowing into a trap?

## Story shape
heist

## Strongest real elements
The golden throne and the scribes waiting to record victory · the slave crossing the dark water · the king believing
every word · the ships too crowded to turn · the Greek battle hymn echoing off the rocks · the first ram strike.

## Beat sheet
- cold_open: dawn, Xerxes on his throne above the strait, a thousand ships waiting
- world: Xerxes is sure: today Greece ends
- complication: the night before, a slave crossed the water with a secret message
- midpoint: Themistocles' message — the Greeks will run, strike now — and Xerxes believes it
- darkest: the Persian fleet rows into the narrow strait at first light
- climax: the ships jam; the Greeks begin to sing
- twist: the bronze rams strike — the message was the trap
- ending: the trap had closed

## Loops
- plant: why is a king watching a battle from a throne? -> payoff: he expects to record a victory, but watches the trap
- plant: what did the secret message say? -> payoff: Themistocles' line, then the trap closing

## Emotional arc
awe (the king, the fleet) → unease (the secret crossing) → tension (the message, the fleet rowing in) → shock (the rams) → irony (the trap closed)

## Sound plan
Dialogue: Xerxes on the throne ("Today, Greece ends."), Themistocles to Sicinnus
Music: opening, the hymn and the rams, ending
